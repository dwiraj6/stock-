Section 01

The problem is calibration, not information

An Indian retail investor deciding whether to put ₹50,000 into a stock has access to an enormous amount of information and almost no calibration.

Every tool in the category outputs confidence — a BUY rating, a target price, a 9/10 score, a “strong fundamentals” badge. Almost none output uncertainty, and none are ever scored against what happened next. The investor has no way to tell a well-founded belief from a confident-feeling one, and no feedback loop that would let them learn the difference.

Why this is hard to fix honestly

The obvious product is a better rating engine. That fails, and this project has the measurements to prove it. Six independent attempts to forecast direction, each scored on point-in-time data the model never saw:

AttemptBrierSkill vs base rate
GBM with estimated drift0.3524−51.0%
Drift shrunk to 50%0.3040−30.3%
Drift shrunk to 25%0.2801−20.1%
Zero drift0.2658−13.9%
Flat +8%/yr — shipped0.2412−3.4%
5 price factors, held-out—−7.0%
The finding the product is built on

None beat the base rate. That is not a limitation to apologise for — it dictates the entire design. A tool that cannot predict direction must be built around what it can measure: the width of the range, and the gap between that and what you believed.

Section 02

Principles

These are load-bearing. Every decision in this document traces to one of them.

#PrincipleWhat it rules out
1Publish the misses in the same size type as the hitsBurying −3.4% in a footnote
2Never produce an unfalsifiable output“You are a Moderate Investor”; a 1–10 rating nobody can score
3A decision is written before the outcome exists, and never revisedRewriting history at signup — see §7.4
4Degrade honestly, never silentlyBlank screens, endless spinners, imputed data
5One moment of motion, then stillnessAmbient animation beside a chart of someone’s money

Section 04

Scope

In scope — shipped

  • Measurement of a single long equity position, NSE-listed
  • Three horizons: 2 months, 6 months, 12 months — lumpsum and 12-month SIP
  • Conviction capture and gap analysis
  • Grounded conversational assistant (ಶಿಷ್ಯ)
  • Personal track record with Brier scoring
  • Risk profiling as constraint-checking
  • Accounts — Google and email/password
  • Published, reproducible validation exhibits

Out of scope

Buy/sell/hold recommendations are permanently out, not “later”. Also out: portfolio construction, options and F&O, order execution, broker integration, mutual funds, bonds, crypto, tax, KYC, and real-time tick data — the product uses delayed and close data by design.

5.2 · Simulation

IDRequirement
S-1Geometric Brownian Motion, 10,000 paths, 252 trading days/year
S-2Seeded mulberry32 PRNG keyed on symbol + IST date — identical output for identical input on the same day
S-3Volatility from adjusted close only; raw OHLC used exclusively for candle rendering
S-4Returns winsorised at the 1st/99th percentile before estimation
S-5Drift is a flat +8%/yr, not estimated per stock — justified by the table in §1 and documented in source
S-6Odds are counted, not derived from percentiles: profit, lose10, lose20, gain20, beatFd
S-7Minimum 250 trading days; below that the payload carries an explicit warning
S-8Requires an authenticated session 🔒

5.6 · Risk profiling

The design call worth defending

Almost every risk profiler asks five questions, adds up points, and announces that you are a Moderate Investor. That output is unfalsifiable — it can never be checked against what happens next — which makes it exactly the kind of unearned confidence Principle 2 exists to forbid.

So there is no label. Four questions collect three constraints, and each is checked against the simulation already running.

IDRequirement
R-1Four questions: purpose, horizon, maximum tolerable loss in ₹, behaviour on a 20% fall
R-2No label is produced.
R-3Each answer is checked against the running simulation and reported as a finding
R-4The behavioural answer is priced: “I would sell” + a counted 8% chance of a 20% fall = “in roughly one future in 13 you would be selling at the bottom”
R-5Findings never say buy, sell, hold or suitable — asserted in the suite
R-6Even the all-clear refuses to imply the stock is a good bet

Section 07

Architecture

Next.js 14.2 App Router · React 18.3 · TypeScript 5.6 · Tailwind v4 · MongoDB Atlas · Gemini · Firebase Auth · Vercel

7.1 · Dependency choices worth defending

ChoiceReasoning
Hand-rolled auth, not Auth.jsNo cryptography is invented — scrypt, CSPRNG, SHA-256, jose for JWKS. What is hand-written is plumbing, which keeps the surface in four readable files. Auth.js v5 is beta and its Credentials provider fits an email-OTP flow badly.
No firebase-adminA Firebase ID token is an ordinary RS256 JWT at a standard JWKS endpoint. jose verifies it in a dozen lines, with no service-account private key to hold or leak.
Plain SMTP, not an email SDKFree tiers of transactional providers only deliver to your own address until a domain is verified — which fails silently for every other user.

7.4 · Storage design — the immutability constraint

Principle 3 dictates two decisions that look odd without it.

  • Decision documents are never rewritten. When an account adopts an anonymous browser history, ownership resolves at read time against a list on the user. Rewriting the owner field on historical rows would make “written before the outcome and never revised” literally false.
  • An anonymous id can be claimed once. The first account to adopt it owns it; every later claim is refused. A leaked id therefore cannot be used to attach to — or steal — a record that already belongs to someone.
Do not “tidy” these

Storage keys were deliberately not renamed when the product became stockಶಿಷ್ಯ. The Mongo database is still plumbline, the session cookie plumbline_session, the anonymous id plumbline.who. Renaming them does not rename anything — it points the app at a new, empty place and orphans every account and every recorded decision. Each carries a source comment saying so.

8.1 · The mascot

ಶಿಷ್ಯ is the plumb bob with a face — not a new character. The bob is already the signature object (the instrument that shows what is actually vertical, after the wobble stops) and is already shaped like a small hooded figure. The hanging cord doubles as a śikhā, the tuft a student traditionally wears.

Five states, driven by real application state: idle · thinking while an answer streams · speaking · pleased for one beat after an answer lands · curious when an observation is waiting. It never loops or idles.

Section 10

Known limitations

Stated because the product’s credibility depends on stating them.

  1. Direction is not predicted, and that is a measured finding across six attempts — not a missing feature.
  2. Survivorship bias. The backtest universe is large-cap NSE names that still trade today; delisted names never entered it.
  3. Overlapping windows. Twelve-month forecasts from six windows are not independent, so the confidence interval is optimistic.
  4. One market regime. A crash inside the test window would change every number in §1.
  5. Fundamentals are not point-in-time. Yahoo serves only today’s P/E and book value, so fundamental factors were excluded from the factor test rather than tested with look-ahead bias.
  6. GBM assumes lognormal returns. Real returns have fatter tails, so the band is, if anything, too narrow at the extremes.
  7. Free-tier ceilings. Gemini allows roughly 100 questions/day across the model chain; Atlas throttles connection bursts.
  8. ALLOW_UNVERIFIED_SIGNUP=1 disables password reset, because nothing can reach a mailbox.

Section 12

Compliance posture

  • Educational tool. Not SEBI-registered investment advice.
  • Recommends no trades; the word “buy” is asserted absent from assistant output.
  • Holds no money; no broker integration; no order path.
  • Stores an email address and nothing else — no phone, no PAN, no KYC.
  • Every screen carries the disclaimer; the assistant restates it on request.
